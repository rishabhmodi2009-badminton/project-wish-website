Who we support

Make-A-Wish Singapore

For children aged 3 to 18

“Together, we create life-changing wishes for children with critical illnesses.”

Grants wishes to children fighting critical illnesses. A wish gives a child something to look forward to during treatment, and something other than illness to talk about at home. Many of these children go on to survive their illness.

makeawish.org.sg → Donate →

Ambulance Wish Singapore

For adults at the end of life

“Fulfilling Wishes, Celebrating Lives.”

Fulfils one final wish for terminally ill adults: a trip to the sea, a family wedding, a last meal somewhere that mattered. We've worked with them since before the club existed.

ambulancewishsingapore.com → Donate →

02

What we actually do

  1. Fundraise

    A Slip 'N Slide booth at the SAS International Fair ($5 for three runs), a dodgeball tournament, and the first social media fundraiser SAS had ever run.

  2. Tell the stories

    We share wish stories at SAS to show students what persevering through hard times looks like. Members also make short videos for Ambulance Wish Singapore's official pages and write awareness pieces for its communications team.

  3. Show up

    Direct service: helping set up wishes before beneficiaries arrive, joining wish trips, and taking photos for the families' memory albums.

  4. Volunteer at events

    Around twenty of us help run Ambulance Wish Singapore's charity golf day each September, plus community outreach events through the year.

How it started

Project Wish began with a stack of posters. In 2024, before there was any club, we put them up in cafés around Robertson Quay for Ambulance Wish Singapore's Cycle of Peace charity ride.

A year later, two club applications, one for Make-A-Wish and one for Ambulance Wish Singapore, merged into a single club. We wanted to show other students that small, ordinary actions can change someone's day, and sometimes the last good day they have.

We also share wish stories at SAS, because they show what it looks like to keep going when things are hard. That lesson matters to students as much as to anyone.

Cycle of Peace posters around Robertson Quay, 2024

  4. 2026

    Rishabh Modi receives the New Service Club Award from the SAS Eagle Service Council at the Honors assembly.

  5. Summer 2026

    A member interns at Ambulance Wish Singapore and builds automations that save its two-person team about three hours a week.

  6. 2026–27

    Storytelling for Ambulance Wish Singapore, a dodgeball tournament, and more members on wish setups.
